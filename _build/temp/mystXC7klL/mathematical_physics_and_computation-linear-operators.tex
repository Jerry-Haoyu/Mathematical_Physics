\subsubsection{Linear Operators}

\paragraph{Change of basis}

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space of dimesnion $n$. Let $\beta=\{e_1,...,e_n\}$ and $\beta'=\{e_1',...,e'_n\}$ be both basis for $V$. We are interested in change reprentation of $x\in V$ from using $\beta$ to $\beta'$. To do this we need to know how $e_j\in \beta$ can be represented using $\beta'$:
\begin{equation}
e_\nu = a_{\nu}^{\mu} e_{\mu}'
\end{equation}
Here coefficientcs $a_{\nu}^{\mu}$ can be orgainized into a matrix. The matrix $a_{\nu}^{\mu}$ looks like:

\begin{equation}
\begin{pmatrix}
a_1^1 & a_2^1 & \cdots & a_n^1\\
\vdots &\vdots  & &\vdots\\
a_n^1 & a_n^1 & \cdots & a_n^1\\
\end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}

\subparagraph{Contravariant Transformation}

There are two interpretations of $a_{\nu}^{\mu}$:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{($a_{\nu}^{\mu}$ transform $\beta'$ to $\beta$)} This is the definition above:

\begin{equation}
e_\nu = a_{\nu}^{\mu} e_{\mu}'
\end{equation}


\item \textbf{($a_{\nu}^{\mu}$ transform representation in $\beta$ to $\beta'$)}
Now for arbitrary $x\in V$, we have:

\begin{equation}
x=c'_{\nu}e'_{\nu}=c_{\nu}e_{\nu} =c_\nu (a_{\nu}^{\mu} e_{\mu}' )=(c_\nu a_{\nu}^{\mu}) e_{\mu}'
\end{equation}


from which we recognize:

\begin{equation}
c_{\nu}'=(c_{\nu}a_{\nu}^{\mu})
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

We note that the matrix has \textit{opposite} effect on basis and representation. This is actually intuitive: in order to represent the vector in new basis $\beta'$, we will have to write the old basis $e_\nu \in \beta$ using linear combinations of the new basis $e_{\mu}'$. The later which effectively transforms basis in $\beta'$ to $\beta$. For this reason, the representation is said to be transformed \textbf{contravariantly}.

\begin{framed}
\textbf{Notation}\\
The matrix $a_{\nu}^{\mu}$ is commonly denoted by $[I]_{\beta}^{\beta'}$ in math textbooks. Therefore:

\begin{itemize}
\item Transform in represenation is denoted by:

\begin{equation}
[x]_{\beta'}=[I]_{\beta}^{\beta'}[x]_{\beta}
\end{equation}


\item Transform in matrix representation of operator is given by:

\begin{equation}
[T]_{\beta}=[I]_{\beta'}^{\beta}[T]_{\beta'}[I]_{\beta}^{\beta'}
\end{equation}
\end{itemize}
\end{framed}
